# Packet 5 - Moments History (VMAs 2022-2025)

Prepared 2026-09-21 for the 2026 MTV Video Music Awards (Sunday 27 September 2026, CBS).

Purpose: honest per-show base rates for live-broadcast bingo squares. The prior pool
over-estimated every tier by 5 to 17 percentage points, so every number below is
deliberately discounted and every gap is marked UNCONFIRMED rather than filled in.

---

## Method and limits (read before using any number)

**Estimation rule applied.** Confirmed in 3 of 3 recent years and structurally built into
the show format: 85-90. Confirmed 3 of 3 but incidental: 75-80. Confirmed 2 of 3: 60-65.
Confirmed 1 of 3: 30-45. Zero confirmed instances but plausible: 8-20. No row is written
above 95, and no row is written at 100.

**Systematic downward bias applied on purpose.** Where a moment is *probably* recurring but
only documented in some years, the rate is set at the confirmed-year fraction, not at the
inferred fraction. Absence of press coverage is not proof the moment did not happen - but a
bingo card should be priced on what a viewer can actually verify on the broadcast, which is
roughly what the press wrote about.

**The single biggest scoring trap.** Many VMA "facts" are true of the winners list but never
appear on the broadcast. Roughly 13 of the 2025 categories were never announced on air; the
winners went up on the VMAs social accounts about 25 minutes after the show. Any square whose
resolution depends on a category being *announced on screen* must be priced far lower than
the same square resolved from the published winners list. Rows below flag this where it bites.

**Research constraints on this packet (material - do not ignore).**
- WebFetch and direct HTTP were unavailable: every candidate domain (en.wikipedia.org,
  nbcnews.com, variety.com, billboard.com, rollingstone.com, hollywoodreporter.com, people.com,
  ew.com, usatoday.com, today.com, cnn.com, time.com, mtv.com, apnews.com and 8 more) returned
  403 at the egress proxy CONNECT tunnel. Nothing was read at source.
- All findings therefore come from WebSearch result summaries plus the URLs those summaries
  cite. The URLs are real and were returned by search; the article bodies were not read directly.
- The session WebSearch budget (200 calls) was exhausted partway through the sweep. Rows that
  were still open at that point are marked UNCONFIRMED, not guessed. See the "open questions"
  list at the end.
- 2022 was treated as the optional fourth year and is the thinnest column by design.

**Show-shape note for 2026.** 2025 was the first CBS simulcast, drew about 5.5 million viewers
(largest VMA audience since 2019), and ran in a network 8-11 p.m. ET slot. 2026 is CBS again.
That matters for three rows: bleeps become more likely and more audible (network standards on a
live feed), hard-slot discipline pushes against a big over-run, and the on-air category count
stays small because the slot is fixed.

## Ten-line summary

1. Most confident, and safe to build a card around: a viral audience reaction shot (90) -
   MTV now produces a dedicated reaction cam, so this is structural rather than lucky.
2. Also high confidence: an artist performs more than one song (95), pyrotechnics (90), a
   Spanish-language or Latin segment (90), a Vanguard medley happening at all (90).
3. High confidence and probably under-rated by the last pool: a category never announced on
   air and dumped to social afterwards (85), and a surprise guest inside a performance (85).
4. Confident with a wording caveat: a profanity bleeped (70), rising to 75 if the square
   accepts a visual cutaway as censorship; CBS network standards in 2026 push this up, not down.
5. Confident that lip-sync discourse follows the show (75) - the 2025 evidence is strongest
   because a performer, Yungblud, said it on the record rather than fans speculating.
6. Least confident, no evidence either way: the host naming a feud or romance (row 12), a pet
   on the carpet (row 28), and any speech being played off (row 4). All three are placeholders.
7. Least confident because the metric is unresolvable: Vanguard speech length (row 15). No
   outlet publishes timings. Recommend cutting this square rather than guessing a number.
8. Actively over-rated by intuition, and the likeliest source of the old pool's 5-17 point
   error: a pregnancy reveal (10), a stage invasion (15), a wardrobe incident (30), a winner
   running across the stage to hug someone (30), and first-and-last-award-same-artist (35).
9. Two rows swing 40+ points on wording alone and must be pinned down before the card is
   printed: row 30 (won in absentia - 85 from the winners list, 40 from the broadcast) and
   row 31 (three or more wins - 85 from the list, 55 on air).
10. Structural note for 2026: 2025 spread its wins across Gaga, Grande, Rose and Carpenter
    rather than concentrating them in one sweeper, so every square that assumes a dominant
    artist should be priced down until the nominations confirm otherwise.
