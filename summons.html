A summons · House of the Dragon, the finale

Swipe

To the lords and ladies of the realm, and to you in particular:

You are summoned to the last hour of the Dance. We watch it together tonight, though we sit in different halls.

A game runs beneath the episode, and it is built so that you need never take your eyes off the TV to play it.

Game One

The Draft

You take your pick of the players in this war — but not just their names. Each of them has several possible fates tonight, and when you claim someone, you choose which of those you are betting on.

So your roster is not a list of people. It is a set of predictions, and no one else at the table will build the same one.

Game Two

The Card

Twenty-five things that might come to pass tonight, dealt to you at random. You tap one the moment you see it.

It is bingo — five in a row and all that — except the squares are things like a dragon dying. Some are near-certain. One or two would put you on your feet.

No two cards are the same, and nobody gets the easy one.

And

Some Guests

Others will be watching with us.

You will know them. That is all you are getting.

And you

Your House

You do not play as a character — you draft those. You come to this table as a house, and that sigil is you for the rest of the night: beside your name on the board, on every pick you make.

Choose carefully. It gets pressed into the wax at the end.

What I need from you

Be In It

Two things I ask, and they pull against each other.

One: actually watch it. The whole hour — not half-watching with your phone face-up. The game runs itself.

Two: agree to stop. We watch each scene through, then break together at the end of it. That is when we get to react.

The stops are not an interruption. They are the night, and we decide how long each one runs.

House Rules

  • We watch each scene all the way through. Nothing stops mid‑scene.
  • At the break we stop together, every TV on the same second.
  • That is when the game catches up — squares claimed, the board settled.
  • And when we react. Shout, argue, call what comes next. This is the point of the whole thing.
  • We start again when we are ready. No timer: a minute, or ten if the scene earned it.

The hard part

Apart, In Sync

We keep this vigil from different houses in different cities, which normally means somebody reacts to a death the rest of us have not reached yet.

There is machinery in the app that holds all of our TVs to the same second and stops and starts us together, so nobody gets spoiled by their own group chat. I am testing it on you.
